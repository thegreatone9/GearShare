Even with Stripe, you **must keep the 'transaction' and 'payment_intent' tables**.

You should never rely on an external API (like Stripe) as your primary database for your application's core logic. Your database remains the "Source of Truth" for your app, while Stripe is the "Source of Truth" for the actual money movement.

Here is why you keep them and how the mapping works.

### 1. Why you keep your tables (The "Mirror" Strategy)

If you deleted your tables and tried to just query Stripe every time you needed data, you would face massive problems:

* **Speed:** Fetching your transaction history would require API calls to Stripe, which are slow compared to querying your own database.
* **Relationships:** Stripe doesn't know about your `rental_id`, `gear_image_url`, or `lender_profile`. You need your local `payment_intents` table to link a financial event to your specific Rental/User data.
* **Searching/Filtering:** You can't easily ask Stripe: *"Show me all transactions for user X involving cameras."* You can easily ask your own SQL database that.

### 2. How the Mapping Works

When you integrate Stripe, you don't replace your data; you **enrich** it. You will create a 1-to-1 mapping between your local records and Stripe's IDs.

#### The `payment_intents` Mapping

You will likely add a column (or use the existing ID column if you switch to strings) to store the **Stripe ID**.

| Your Column | Value (Before Stripe) | Value (After Stripe Integration) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`id`** (or `stripe_id`) | `uuid-1234` (Generated by you) | **`pi_3MtwPdLkdIw...`** (Generated by Stripe) |
| `status` | `AUTHORIZED` | `requires_capture` (Mapped from Stripe webhook) |
| `amount` | `200.00` | `20000` (Stripe uses cents) |

**The Workflow:**

1. **App:** Calls `stripe.paymentIntents.create({ amount: 20000 })`.
2. **Stripe:** Returns an ID: `pi_3Mtw...`.
3. **App:** Inserts into your `payment_intents` table: `id = 'pi_3Mtw...', status = 'AUTHORIZED'`.

#### The `transactions` Mapping

Similarly, when a charge succeeds or a refund happens, Stripe generates an ID.

| Your Column | Value (Before Stripe) | Value (After Stripe Integration) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`stripe_txn_id`** | *NULL* | **`ch_3MtwPd...`** (Charge ID) or **`re_123...`** (Refund ID) |
| `type` | `SECURITY_DEPOSIT` | (Logic you infer based on the event) |

### 3. The Sync Mechanism (Webhooks)

The magic that keeps your tables in sync with Stripe is called a **Webhook**.

1. **Event:** A payment succeeds on Stripe's side.
2. **Stripe:** Sends a background signal (Webhook) to your API: *"Hey, payment intent `pi_3Mtw...` just succeeded."*
3. **Your API:**
* Finds the row in `payment_intents` where `id = 'pi_3Mtw...'`.
* Updates the local status to `CAPTURED`.
* Inserts a new row into `transactions` to record the event.

### Summary

Your tables act as the **local cache** and **relational bridge**.

* **Stripe** holds the raw financial truth (credit card details, bank transfers).
* **Your DB** holds the business logic truth (who rented what, and which payment ID paid for it).

So, keep your schema exactly as it is. When you integrate Stripe, you will simply start filling your `id` columns with Stripe's IDs instead of your own random UUIDs.
